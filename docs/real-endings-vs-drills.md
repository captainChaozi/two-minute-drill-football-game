# Real-game endings vs original drills

Two ways to play [2 Minute Drill](https://2mindrillgame.org/), summarised from the site's [rules and model](https://2mindrillgame.org/rules-and-model) page.

| Question | Real-game endings | Original drills |
|---|---|---|
| Where it starts | Recent completed games and 20 archived 2023 snapshots | 6 fictional training situations |
| When it ends | End of regulation, including the opponent's possessions | After one offensive possession |
| Who calls defence | The opponent picks its plays; you manage your defensive timeouts | A simplified simulated defensive look |
| Feedback | Descriptive decision review | Rule-based teaching grade and practice target |

## Shared rules

- Field goals are available within the game's 65-yard limit; make rates are game estimates.
- A touchdown adds six points and a field goal three; real endings can offer a one-point kick or a two-point try.
- A tied regulation finish is not shown as a win. Overtime is not simulated.

## Data

Historical starts come from public nflverse play-by-play (CC BY 4.0). The site checks for new completed games every 15 minutes; a game becomes playable only when its final score and play-by-play agree.

## Which to pick

- Learning downs and field position: original drills with the coach.
- Testing decisions under a real score and clock: today's real ending.
